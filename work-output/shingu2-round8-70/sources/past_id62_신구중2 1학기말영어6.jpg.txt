 Viral Marketing
 Wait, Yuna! You only want the T-shirt because you saw it again and again on social media. It isn’t just you. There are a lot of people who ① falls for a viral marketing strategy. Why? Information about a product can spread quickly and widely on the Internet, just like a “virus.” If a product ⑤ become hot on social media, people naturally want to have it. Yuna, just remember that a popular ③ products isn’t always right for you.
 Anchoring Effect
 Hold on, Somi! Your budget is 50 dollars, but you are buying a 60-dollar lipstick. The 60-dollar lipstick sounds cheap only because the salesperson ④ shows you a 70-dollar lipstick first. This is an example of the anchoring effect. Usually, the first piece of information becomes an “anchor” and ⑥ influences the shopper’s decision. In your case, the 70-dollar lipstick was the anchor. Somi, don’t just rely on the first piece of information that is given to you.
